# Register and Engagement

**How MGA is written, and how it is developed in public.**

This document states the register MGA is written in and the rule that governs everything said about
it publicly during development. It contains no story spoilers.

It exists because MGA is a long project. A game that takes years to make has to answer a question
that a short one never faces: **what is the relationship with the people who are paying attention the
whole time?** This document is that answer, and it is a commitment, not an aspiration — the binding
form is [Manifesto Principle 16 — Never Sell the Payoff](manifesto.md#16-never-sell-the-payoff).

---

## 1. The Register

In 2008, Post's Canadian cereal brand Shreddies rotated its square product 45° and marketed the
result as *Diamond Shreddies* — a new shape, presented completely deadpan, at full production values,
eventually reaching a combo pack containing both squares and diamonds. Nobody was fooled. Nobody was
meant to be. It ran for a long time and it worked.

Three properties of that campaign describe how MGA is made:

**Enormous sincere investment behind a premise that does not need it.** The commitment *is* the
point. Work done far past the level the joke requires stops being a joke about cereal and becomes an
act of good faith.

**The audience is a co-conspirator, not a mark.** The pleasure is being on the inside of something
performed with a straight face. The moment an audience is being *worked*, the whole thing collapses.

**The affection is displaced into the construction.** Nothing is stated directly. The effort is the
statement.

That last one is the register. It is reticence expressed as work: feeling routed through
infrastructure because the direct channel is closed. It is not the absence of feeling. It is the
opposite, spent differently.

---

## 2. What the Register Means for the Writing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Understatement over emphasis** | The biggest things get the smallest lines |
| **Apology as punctuation** | Reflexive, social, not a sign of weakness |
| **Deferral of the direct statement** | Characters route feeling through action and favour |
| **Long setups held straight** | The story will let a thing run past where it is comfortable |
| **Payoff as inclusion** | The reader is *let in*, rather than shown up |

MGA's protagonist is a retired magical girl trying to disappear into an ordinary life. She will not
announce what she is feeling. That is not an accident of characterisation and it is not a problem to
be written around — it is the house style, applied at the level of a person.

### English (Canada) is the source register, not a spelling variant

MGA's default locale is **en-CA**. This is not a decision about `colour` and `metre`.

The Canadian register above is the language the game is *written in*. Every other locale is a
translation of a Canadian text. Any future en-US pass is a localisation of this, not the origin — and
specifically, an American edit tends to want the punchline earlier and the feeling said out loud.
That is a translation note to be resisted, in the same way that flattening any other source register
would be.

## 3. Worked Example: Project Tangent

MGA's public development codename is **Project Tangent**. The derivation:

```
OGM / MGA   →   cancel the GM   →   O / A   →   tan θ
```

*Ordinary Girl Meghan over Magical Girl Ava*, treated as a fraction, cancelled, leaving opposite over
adjacent — the tangent. The fraction is also the logo.

This is a joke nobody asked for, carrying real naming cost, deployed across streams and dev videos
for years, whose entire return is the moment a viewer works it out. It is the register in miniature,
and it predates this document — the shape was being made before it had a name.

## 4. The Long Cycle

MGA is developed by one person and it will take years.

That is stated plainly rather than hedged, because the alternative — implying a nearer horizon and
revising it repeatedly — is the beginning of the failure described in the next section. Systems are
built in the order that de-risks the project, not in the order that would make any single part look
finished soonest. Some parts of the game will sit visibly unfinished for a very long time. That is
the correct steady state for work of this shape, not slippage.

The register makes this coherent rather than merely tolerable. Years of sincere investment toward
something the audience gets to be part of is a recognised mode. It is the mode this project is in.

---

## 5. The Discipline — Distributed Payoff, Not Deferred Payoff

Everything above is attractive, and therefore dangerous. It can be misread as permission to defer,
and that misreading is the one that kills long projects with strong communities.

### The failure mode: promise debt

If a years-long relationship with an audience is built on *"there is a large payoff coming,"* then
every month of that relationship accrues a debt denominated in something that does not exist yet. The
audience is not in on anything — they are holding an IOU. Expectations compound. The imagined product
outgrows anything that could be built. The eventual release is measured against the fantasy rather
than against itself, and it loses, no matter what it is.

That failure converts an audience into **creditors**. It is the exact opposite of a co-conspirator,
and it is the specific outcome this project intends to avoid.

### The correction

**Diamond Shreddies was funny immediately.** There was no withheld reveal — the pleasure was watching
the straight face be maintained, and that was available from the first week and available again every
week after. The payoff was never at the end. It was distributed across the whole run.

> **The rotation is visible early. The reason for it can take years.**

Applied here: everything MGA says publicly during development — dev logs, dev videos, design
documents, streams — must be **a complete thing on its own**, worth someone's time even if MGA never
ships. This is why the design documentation is public and written to be readable rather than
extracted, and why the dev video series explains general practice rather than teasing MGA content.

Nothing gets spent as a promissory note against the finished game.

### The test

Any public-facing thing this project makes has to pass three questions:

1. **Does it pay out now?** If its value depends on a future release, it is debt, not a beat.
2. **Is the audience in on something, or waiting for something?** In-on is an asset. Waiting is a
   liability.
3. **Would this still have been worth making if the game were cancelled tomorrow?** If yes, it is
   part of the work. If no, it is an IOU.

A consequence worth stating: this project will not run pre-order campaigns, countdowns to
announcements, or roadmaps with dates it cannot hold. Those are all instruments for borrowing against
a payoff.

## 6. Publishing and Monetisation During Development

Section 5 is a rule about what the project *says*. This section is the same rule applied to what the
project *charges for* and *where it posts*, because a payoff can be sold as easily as it can be
promised.

MGA posts development in three places: the public dev board, Patreon, and SubscribeStar.

### Free tiers receive everything

**There is no paywalled development content.** Dev reports go out in full to every tier on both
platforms, and the public dev board requires no account at all.

This is not generosity, it is consistency. Section 5's test asks whether a public thing pays out now
or is an IOU. A dev report placed behind a paid tier answers that question badly in both directions:
it is *still* an IOU, and now it has a price.

### Billing pauses until there is something worth paying for

While the project's entire public output is writing about a game, **billing stays paused** — patrons
may subscribe, and are not charged.

The reasoning is the promise-debt argument with money attached. Accepting payment for reports about
an unfinished game is borrowing against the finished one, at the worst possible interest rate: it
converts the most engaged part of the audience into creditors *and* gives them a receipt. Being on
the inside of something built in public should cost nothing until the thing being built can be
installed.

Billing resumes when there is a build worth putting on a hard drive. **That is a description of a
state, not a date** — a date would be exactly the kind of roadmap commitment Section 5 refuses.

### Adult images: two editions, never two tiers

MGA is an adult game, and once dev updates begin showing character work, some images will not be
postable everywhere.

When that starts, **each update ships as two editions covering the same development** — one with the
adult images, one without.

Three constraints on that, all of which exist to stop the split from becoming a paywall by
accident:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Same development in both** | The editions differ only in which images are attached. Neither is a summary of the other. |
| **Not a tier** | Both editions are free. Edition is a function of *where it is being posted* and what the reader has opted into, never of what they pay. |
| **Not a reveal** | The adult edition is not a withheld payoff, and it is never advertised as one. |

This mirrors the game's own architecture — a base that is safe by construction, with adult content as
a separately installed thing you opt into. Building the game that way and then running the dev
updates differently would be incoherent.

## 7. What This Does Not License

Each of these is a plausible misreading, so each is refused explicitly:

- **It does not license withholding.** The joke is visible from the start. Only the reason is slow.
- **It does not license coyness in the writing.** Deferral is a character behaviour and a structural
  rhythm — not a licence for scenes that refuse to resolve.
- **It does not license slowness as a virtue.** The long cycle is defensible because each beat pays
  out along the way, not because taking a long time is itself admirable.
- **It is not a comedy mandate.** "Joke" here means *constructed shared thing*, not humour. The
  register is deadpan sincerity, and a great deal of MGA's material is not funny at all.
- **It is not a licence to under-deliver.** Refusing to sell the payoff is not the same as not
  building one.
- **It is not a vow of poverty.** The paused billing in Section 6 is a statement about *when* payment
  is earned, not a position that the work should be free. The pause ends when there is a product.
